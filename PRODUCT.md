# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Early is primarily for internet tastemakers and early fans: people who discover creators, ideas, posts, music, videos, and communities before broad attention arrives and want a credible record of that discovery.

Creators and brands are secondary users who can recognize, reward, and build campaigns for genuinely early supporters. Developers are a secondary audience who can use Early's proof providers and privacy-preserving reputation layer inside their own products.

## Product Purpose

Early turns previously invisible moments of online discovery into portable, verifiable proof. The live proof of concept lets someone paste an X post that their account both liked and replied to, verify the interaction from authenticated platform data, and create a proof of when they were there.

The wider product is not limited to X. Early is intended to support discovery across platforms such as YouTube, Instagram, Spotify, and GitHub, then let people build a private cross-platform history of what they found early. Success means people understand the value immediately, can try the working X proof without friction, and can see credible uses for fans, creators, brands, and developers.

## Positioning

Early is proof of discovery for the internet: it makes being there before the crowd verifiable without turning a person's complete discovery history into a public feed.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: paste the URL of an X post the visitor both liked and replied to, then create a proof immediately.
- Secondary CTA: understand how Early works and explore how the protocol serves fans, creators, brands, and developers.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: You knew before everyone else. Early lets you prove it.
- Belief ladder: early discovery has cultural value; screenshots and personal claims are weak evidence; authenticated platform data can prove the interaction; private computation can make a broader discovery history useful without exposing all of it; this can support reputation, recognition, rewards, campaigns, and developer products.
- Proof on hand: a working X proof flow using Reclaim's zkTLS infrastructure and a private ranking experiment using Zama FHE. Reclaim and Zama may appear as technologies Early uses, not as endorsements or partnerships. Stellar, hackathon participation, and deployed Stellar contracts are not part of the public landing-page credibility story.

## Brand Personality

Exciting, clean, and authored. Early should feel culturally aware and technically credible without speaking like a developer tool on every screen. The experience should create the feeling of recognizing a small signal before it grows, then preserving it as something valuable.

## Anti-references

Do not make Early look like a generic Web3 landing page, a fake analytics dashboard, or an AI-generated stack of feature cards. Avoid purple gradients, decorative glass panels, repetitive three-column card rows, abstract protocol diagrams without product meaning, and empty technical spectacle. The landing page should not imply that X is the whole product or that infrastructure providers are commercial partners.

## Design Principles

1. Make the product idea visible before explaining the technology.
2. Let visitors try the working proof flow at the moment their interest is highest.
3. Treat X as the first demonstration of a cross-platform idea, not the boundary of the brand.
4. Give fans emotional relevance and give creators, brands, and developers concrete utility.
5. Explain privacy through what remains hidden and what becomes possible, not through cryptography jargon alone.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Use semantic structure, keyboard-operable controls, visible focus states, sufficient contrast, readable text measures, and touch-friendly targets. Preserve the project's reduced-motion treatment so the full narrative remains understandable when scroll choreography and atmospheric motion are disabled.
